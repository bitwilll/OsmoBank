HOW YOUR DETAILS ARE PROTECTED
lock
Sealed before storage
Your name, date of birth, document number and address are encrypted with AES-256-GCM in the request that carries them. The database only ever holds ciphertext.
verified_user
Tamper-evident
GCM authenticates as well as encrypts. A record that has been altered fails to open — it cannot quietly decrypt to something else.
visibility_off
The queue is blind
Reviewers triage from initials, country and document type. Nothing is decrypted to build the list — only when one case is deliberately opened.
receipt_long
Every read is logged
Opening a submission writes an audit entry naming the reviewer, the case and the time. Withdrawing destroys the sealed record outright.
infoWHAT THIS DOES NOT CLAIM
Osmo Assure is not end-to-end encrypted. A reviewer has to read your documents to verify them, so the server necessarily holds the key. What encryption buys you is that a stolen database dump is useless on its own, and that no part of the app can show your details by accident. Anyone who controls both the database and the key material could read submissions — which is why every read is audited, and why a deployment handling real identity documents should keep the key outside the database entirely. The line below says which way this deployment is configured.
